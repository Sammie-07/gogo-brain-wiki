# From TikTok Home Tours to Real Estate Leads (Nadia, GoGetEm)

> [[GoGet'Em Community]] training (Nov 10, 2026, ~44 min), hosted by Natalia. Guest Nadia (surname not in the source), a GGTC member and eXp agent in Cape Coral, Florida, who works relocation buyers, moved from Germany in 2015, was licensed in 2021 and has 10K+ TikTok followers. [[Gogo Bethke]] added the price-point and "you-language" lessons. Her results are her own.

## Key Takeaways

### Faceless home tours (folds into [[Social Media and Email Marketing]])
- Built for introverts: no face on camera, "the house is the star." She previews homes anyway, so she films them; out-of-state buyers can't drive around, so they want exactly this.
- **One city, one home type, one price band.** Pool homes at $340–400K; waterfront/golf-access at $500–995K (staying under $1M, a different niche). The cheapest homes got views but no real buyers and went under contract in a day or two. Buyers who start at $500K often end at $700–800K, so mix prices a little.
- **Batching:** on Sunday, pick 6–7 vacant homes (no owners, no pets) from a saved MLS search and tour them back to back the next day (3–4 hours). Post one a day Monday to Friday; edit each in the TikTok app the day it posts (5–10 minutes). Total about 4.5–5 hours a week.
- **Filming:** lights on, blinds open, start with the back shot (pool or view), then 3–5-second clips through the house. She films 20–25 clips and uses 10–15, keeps it under a minute, adds TikTok music and one transition applied to all. Cover text is only city, home type and price. Tried voiceovers and dropped them.
- **Hook test:** "What you get for $X in Cape Coral" got more views but off-topic comments; "Cape Coral, Florida · golf-access/pool home · price" got fewer views and better buyers. Test in your market.
- Never post the address; viewers comment a keyword ("pool," "boat") or DM to get it. Mention details that spark questions (e.g. flood zone X).

### From DM to CRM (folds into [[Lead Generation]])
- In the DM, **ask for an email first, to deliver the home they asked about plus similar ones.** "Deliver first." No qualifying questions: "I didn't pitch anything. I asked." People volunteer timing, reasons and travel dates.
- Move every email into the CRM (Follow Up Boss) with a **saved search** emailed weekly from her IDX site; the site asks for a phone number after three homes, so she never has to.
- Screenshot DM threads, have AI summarize them, paste the summary into the CRM. Note travel dates and reach out two to three weeks before their next visit.
- Get leads out of TikTok fast: usernames like "user12345" are impossible to track.
- **Her 2026 numbers:** 139 emails, 88 phone numbers, 25 warm and 6 hot leads, 2 signed buyer agreements, 2 closings for $44K GCI. Relocation converts in 3–18 months. "Followers are nice, but the conversations pay my bills."
- **Start-over plan:** pick the price range on day one and set up the follow-up (DM questions, email, CRM steps) before the first video. Week 1: one city, one home type, one price range, caption and keyword. Week 2: book and film 6–7 vacant homes, post, answer every DM, ask for the email. Week 3: every lead into the CRM with a saved search and an immediate thank-you.

### Gogo's additions
- **Price attracts the client.** "When you post a $100,000 home, what type of buyer do you find? $100,000 buyers." 3% of $100K is $3,000; of $1M is $30,000. "Is the work the same? Yep. Is the pay the same? Nope." She once avoided posting million-dollar homes because she didn't feel comfortable with millionaires.
- **Narrow the MLS search to one ideal client.** A $200–500K swing can hold two different buyers (e.g. new construction at $200K and golf communities at $400–500K); run them as separate tour days.
- **Automate DMs from day one.** With almost 100K Instagram followers plus Facebook, TikTok and YouTube, even 10–20 messages a day unanswered wastes the marketing. Set up TikTok as a business account early if it unlocks automation.
- **"I-language vs you-language."** Her brain writes from her own experience ("just wait until you see the kitchen"); buyers care about themselves ("Can you imagine yourself in this kitchen?"). She has AI rewrite her ideas into you-language and hooks.
