# The Circle Coaching — Agent-Attraction Video, Enrollment Calls, Speaking & Team Leadership (de-identified)

> Private Circle group-coaching call led by [[Gogo Bethke]] (application-only mastermind format). **NDA — raw transcript not retained; all member/team/vendor names, locations, and deal specifics stripped.** Only Gogo's generalizable teachings are captured here. Themes: building an agent-attraction video + landing page, booking and running enrollment calls, the free-training-as-bait funnel, structuring speaking engagements, getting on podcasts, hiring/leading a support team, and account-security hygiene for scaling.

## Key Takeaways

### Agent-attraction video + landing page
- Record a simple, horizontal, YouTube-style intro video — don't over-edit or over-script. Clean, uncluttered, quiet room; soft natural window light, never direct sun. Can't read a teleprompter? Talk from the heart off a few pillars: (A) why you made your move, (B) why someone should choose *you*.
- **Segment the audience and name each segment in the video:** brand-new/struggling agents ("I'll make you a producing agent"), established producers with no way to earn fees back (icon/cap-back program), and top producers ready to build a team. Position yourself as someone who's walked each stage: *"just like you have an ideal client, you have an ideal agent."*
- End with **one clear CTA** ("let's talk — book my calendar"). Layer it: short personal intro video up top, then a longer "watch this next" video explaining the full model (cap, splits, revenue share) so the first stays short.

### The enrollment call
- Booking-link rules: (1) **video call, not phone** ("much harder to say no to someone's face"); (2) **45 minutes** ideal (15 can't cover model + numbers + objections); (3) title it for what it is ("Let's talk [opportunity]"), not a vague "coaching call."
- Blunt filtering description: for licensed agents who intend to join and name you as sponsor; must watch all videos first; **no-shows won't be rescheduled** ("if you have no consideration for my time, we don't need to be business partners"). Letting low-character people weed themselves out up front is why the team has "no drama."
- Closing move: ask **"By when are you planning to make this decision?"** to commit them to a timeline. Agents ready *now* get the recorded full presentation immediately, then the call; those near month-end get invited to the live.

### Free-training-as-bait funnel
- Run a recurring members-only training that **opens to the public once a month** as a low-commitment "appetizer" / "try the shoes on," paired with a longer recorded model-explainer for the ready.
- At the open training, state: "whoever invited you would love for you to attend." **The inviter follows up — the leader never chases guests** (leader helps via a three-way call but doesn't track others' results). Don't post the public link directly — have prospects **DM you for it** so you have a list of exactly who raised their hand.
- Skip live recruiting events right before major holidays. Newer partners keep booking **three-way calls** (leader does the heavy lifting) until they can pitch solo; keep a running list of agents you'd love to partner with, including great agents you meet across your own transactions.

### Co-sponsorship mechanics (generalized)
- The joining agent inherits the **sponsor's** entire upline/benefits; a co-sponsor passes down only themselves. So to give a recruit your full upline, **be the sponsor, not the co-sponsor**.
- You don't co-sponsor with anyone already in your downline (they inherit everything anyway), and you **can't co-sponsor with someone in your own downline**. With a partner, alternate sponsor/co per deal. The richer your upline's benefits, the more agents want you as sponsor. (See [[Co-Sponsorship and Agent Attraction System]] for full mechanics.)

### Using numbers as social proof
- Post **actual numbers**, not adjectives — "best year of my career" is meaningless; "2 deals" vs "2,200 deals" is everything. Publish **collective/group production stats** to signal the caliber of the room ("you have to earn your way into that room") — which also carries you during a personally off year. Rounded lifetime numbers are fine.

### Speaking engagements
- Ask the host two questions first: **how long** and **what topic**. Go deep on **one** topic, not a hundred. Draft the deck with AI: give it the topic, the length, and **"one slide per minute"** ("a minute on stage is like holding a plank"). **Bullets only, no paragraphs** — you fill in the story out loud. Structure: authority-earning intro (striking stat + your relevant credential) → body → clear CTA. If you can't sell from stage, **drive people to your social** instead.

### Getting on podcasts
- Make a dedicated 1-hour "podcast" booking link (for bigger shows, use their calendar). Write a **podcast bio** ("why interview me": achievements first, then personality). Build a **templated outreach email** the assistant sends in their own voice. Source shows from your brokerage's communities, its own show, state groups, and Google — "you're not doing any of this, your team is."

### Hiring & leading a support team
- Leaders usually aren't organized by nature — **hire people whose gift is organizing you**, and have your ops lead study/implement a team operating-system framework (know enough to be dangerous yourself).
- Hire for **critical thinking**, not task-robots — you want "should we do A, B, or C?" not "what do I do?" Standard: *"if I can do your job, you have no job security — be so good I can't figure out how you do it."* **Cross-train every role** so nothing stalls when someone's out.
- **"Try three times before you escalate":** (1) Google it, (2) ask AI, (3) ask a teammate — only then bring it to the leader. Coach people to think by asking "what do *you* think the right answer is?" then affirm the reasoning. Manage partners' assistants to your own standard and nip underperformance early ("your hourly rate is too high to repeat yourself"); lead by example.

### Account / password security (for scaling)
- Before sharing any working-email password with your team, **create a brand-new email only you control** and switch all financial/banking/credit-card **password-reset destinations** to it — only then hand over the working email. (Or keep your personal email private and forward system mail to a shared inbox.)
- **Caution:** connecting an AI assistant to an inbox that still receives banking reset codes effectively gives it access to those accounts. Audit what your automations are connected to.
