# The Circle Coaching — Peer-Led Calls, the Attraction Page, Downline Community & Delegation Rules (de-identified)

> Private [[The Circle|Circle]] group-coaching call led by [[Gogo Bethke]]. **NDA: raw transcript not retained; all member, team, vendor and location details removed.** Only Gogo's reusable teaching is kept. Themes: how the Circle call runs (including member-led sessions), what an agent attraction page must do, how to set up a downline community, text-replacement speed habits, delegation rules for assistants, the "open tabs" system, asset protection, and tax planning for high earners.

## Key Takeaways

### How the Circle call runs
- The call keeps a fixed day and time. If Gogo is travelling, a member leads that session and her team still attends, rather than moving or cancelling it. Members are all top producers, so each has something to teach.
- Accept the host's original calendar invite. A copy you make yourself won't update when the host changes it. Gogo quotes times in ET and the invite converts them.
- Members add their own assistants to the Circle chat, so assistants get answers there instead of asking their boss.
- Between calls, members can ask Gogo's AI "second brain" (the Chat with Gogo button on their dashboard) and report any odd answers.

### Speed habit: text replacement
- Save every link you send often (attraction page, booking link, website, Zoom) as a phone text replacement: Settings → Text Replacement → + → paste the link as the phrase, two letters as the shortcut. Gogo's "pp" sends her partner page and "mm" her team map in seconds.
- Alternative: one shared "Links" note your assistant keeps current. No codes to remember.

### The agent attraction page
- Required: (1) a video on who you are and why partner with you, about what *they* gain, not your life story; (2) your achievements in numbers; (3) one page with three steps: watch the videos → book a call → sign up. After the call, send them back to the same page.
- Name the booking calendar "Let's Talk eXp", never "free coaching call" or "30 minutes with [name]", which attract brain-pickers and product pitches. 30 minutes to start.
- Add a team application form that texts you and your assistant on submit, and asks for their birthday, so late sign-ups don't get lost.
- Don't gate the videos behind a lead form unless you will actually follow up. Gogo collected contacts for years and never did.
- Show a platform-vs-multiplier comparison (brokerage benefits vs. your organization's extra benefits) and link to public events and the referral map.
- Co-sponsors build their page once and send all later edits through Gogo's support team. A new site per edit creates duplicates.

### Attraction cadence and positioning
- Post attraction content at least once a week. Every other week isn't enough.
- Move from production into attraction gradually; agents want to learn from someone still active.
- Gogo prefers recruiting nationwide: local agents feel you're taking their deals.
- Build a weekly habit that puts you with agents (for example a regular golf foursome: three prospects a week).
- Product ladder: free downline organization → low-ticket recurring digital product → high-ticket coaching. If your upline already runs good weekly trainings, send people there and add your own monthly session.

### Downline community architecture
See [[Downline Community Architecture]] for the full structure: a Facebook group for announcements plus Messenger chats split into frontline, general and topic sub-chats; personal birthday celebrations; access only for sponsored agents.

### Delegation: don't be your assistant's assistant
- *"You are not their assistant. They are yours."* Never fetch things for your assistant; they can't afford your hourly rate.
- Question order: Google it or ask an AI → ask a teammate → only then the leader. Tech questions go to the weekly tech-support call.
- Send a resource twice at most; after that they store it themselves.
- If you train staff to bring every question to you, they still will in 10 years.
- Reading DISC: Gogo only needs the result bars (tall/medium/short) to match a person to a role, not their name or video; true colours show in 3–6 months.
- Need help now? A VA placement company has pre-screened candidates who can start next week. Once you have one or two VAs, they hire the next from a step-by-step booklet.

### The "open tabs" system
- Every unfinished task is an open tab in your head; too many means stress. Tasks get assigned Monday ("is Friday reasonable?"), blockers surface Wednesday, work is done Friday. Gogo doesn't care when the work happens, only that it's done, but is exacting on details like spelling and email formatting.
- Build the team around how you work best; stay close early, then step back as trust grows.

### Asset protection additions
- Split similar assets across more than one holding company (by state or asset type) so one lawsuit can't reach everything; ask your CPA when to separate.
- Write a family blueprint (every property, LLC, holding company and the trust) so heirs aren't overwhelmed.
- Property management: if you enjoy self-managing local properties, fine, but never give up 90% profit to avoid a ~10% fee. Out-of-state portfolios need professional management.

### Tax planning for high earners
- Hold quarterly meetings with a "financial board" (CPA, financial advisor, tax attorney, business advisor). Project next quarter's income and act before year-end, for example by buying an asset if income will outrun deductions.
- Standard agent tax advice is built for average incomes.
- Read the fine print on tax-credit advance or loan products: repayment can come due if the credit is denied or delayed.

### Mindset
- *"If is a 50/50 chance. When is a matter of time."* Say "when".
- The private-chef story: Gogo assumed a chef cooks a set menu, then learned you can ask for anything. Habits limit what you can imagine, which is why everyone needs a coach.
- A big tax bill means a strong year. Problems like these show you've reached a level most people quit before.
