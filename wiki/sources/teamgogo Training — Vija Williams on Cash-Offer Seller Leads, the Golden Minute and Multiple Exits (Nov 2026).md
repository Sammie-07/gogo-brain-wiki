# #teamgogo Training — Vija Williams on Cash-Offer Seller Leads, the Golden Minute and Multiple Exits (Nov 2026)

> Weekly [[#teamgogo]] Wednesday training (Nov 4, 2026, ~66 min), hosted by [[Kristy Waker]]. Guest [[Vija Williams]] (Realtor.com Chief Growth Officer) taught how to convert cash-offer seller leads; [[Jason Dupree]] gave the mortgage minute; [[Nick Krem]] covered [[Halo]]. Gogo did not teach on this call. Vija sells these leads through Realtor.com and said so; her figures are her own.

## Key Takeaways

### From first call to last opinion (folds into [[Lead Generation]] / [[AI for Real Estate]])
- Agents used to be the seller's first call and the first to show them comps. Now sellers research first: about 64% use AI before meeting an agent, and three-quarters of those ask it about price (Zillow Consumer Home Trends study, per Vija). The agent's new role is interpreter of data and trusted consultant.
- Sellers' top trait in an agent: trust (80%), then local expert knowledge, then pricing; commission ranked fifth.
- 68% (Zillow) to 80% (NAR) of sellers hire the first agent they meet: "You don't have to be best, you have to be first."
- **The formula:** get in front of as many homeowners as possible, as early as possible, and nurture them. "Simple, not easy."
- Her 2008–09 Seattle lesson: her brokerage told her to avoid short sales and foreclosures, and she missed that market. She calls early-stage seller leads today's equivalent.

### Cash-offer leads (folds into [[Lead Generation]])
- A "high-intent, early-stage" lead: a homeowner who filled out a form asking for a cash offer. The real value is talking to a seller before they were ready to call an agent.
- About 25% transact within 18 months (it was ~30%); about 10% start around day 62. Only ~5% actually take a cash offer, so ~95% are really listing leads.
- Expected conversion 5–10%, versus ~1% for internet buyer leads. She suggests ~10 a month to build toward a listing a month after a 2–4 month ramp.
- The form captures the reason for selling and the timeline (ASAP, 0–3, 3–6 months). Use leads with documented TCPA opt-in; with signed consent, AI prospecting can be used on them.
- Sources: buy them (~$100 each, ~$1,000/month for a meaningful volume), make your own with direct mail ($5–10K per drop, works but costly) or Meta ads, or through eXp's FastCap (complete all 4 weeks for a year of shared "jump" leads, hard to claim in crowded markets).
- **Why sellers ask for a cash offer:** need for speed, financial hardship (a spectrum, from insecure to missing payments), condition of the home, relocation, inherited property, privacy.

### Speed to lead and the call (folds into [[Lead Generation]] / [[Communication]])
- **The golden minute:** people leave a site within about 60 seconds. Reaching a lead in 60–120 seconds gives about a 90% contact rate; otherwise expect 10–40 attempts (call, text, email, once or twice a day) over 10 days. If you won't do that, don't buy leads of any kind.
- Train family or helpers to set appointments and pay per appointment.
- **The only win on a first call** is a face-to-face appointment with a qualified seller, ideally same day or within 24–72 hours; every hour of delay raises the cancel/ghost risk. Never give a cash-offer number on the phone.
- **Don't open with your brokerage**; the seller didn't request an agent. Open by responding to their request: "Hi, this is [name], I'm calling about the cash offer you requested. Is 123 Main Street the home you're looking to sell?"
- Calls run 3.5–6 minutes; past 9 minutes you're having the appointment on the phone.
- **Pre-qual questions:** (1) When are you planning to sell? (2) Why are you selling? (3) What matters more: the most money, or getting it done fast with certainty? (4) Only for possible cash-offer candidates: how much do you owe and how much do you want?
- At the house, sit down first and ask them again: in person you get the real answer.

### Multiple exits (folds into [[Negotiation]])
- The post-AI agent can't show up with only "list it or don't." Package and label 4–6 programs:
  1. Traditional retail listing.
  2. As-is investor special: on the MLS and marketed to investors at the same time, sometimes with limited showings (e.g. a "hybrid" program).
  3. Renovation paid at closing (eXp's concierge program, up to $50K); charge more if you manage the work.
  4. iBuyer partner that keeps the agent in the deal: Homeward or Zudelio (eXp's Express Offers). Sellers get an initial check, then an "upside" check after the resale listing.
  5. Local investors and builders (swift exits, rent-backs).
- Multiple exits are also strong blog and AI-search content.
- **Presenting a low cash offer** to a non-candidate: put yourself on the seller's side against the offer ("investors buy at wholesale so they can sell at retail"), and note that other cash offers will come in about the same. For real candidates, be fully transparent about the buy and resale prices. Serving the seller's goal can mean speed over the most money.

### Follow-up (folds into [[Lead Generation]] / [[Communication]])
- "Your gold is in your follow-up." Nurture forever, but get the in-person meeting first: nurture works far better after an eyeball-to-eyeball meeting.
- Always give them something to respond to (a nearby sale that affects their value), not "just checking in." Mix text, email and occasional voice messages.
- Ryan Serhant's story: he followed up every other week for 5 years with no reply, then sold that buyer over $100M of property.
