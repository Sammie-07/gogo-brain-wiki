# ChatGPT Controlling Your Browser — Social Media, CRM and Paperwork (Nathan, GoGet'Em)

> [[GoGet'Em Community]] training (~1h23m). Guest [[Nathan (Synthetic Echo podcast)|Nathan]], a former Toronto-area broker turned full-time AI educator, shows how a ChatGPT (or Claude) Chrome extension drives the sites you're logged into: MLS, Skyslope, CRM, Instagram, LinkedIn. [[Gogo Bethke]] adds how to build the habit; [[Kristy Waker]] adds a note on how the extension works.

## Key Takeaways

### Train AI like a new hire (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]])
- **Treat AI like an assistant, not technology.** Give each bot a name, a job and a personality. Expect ~90–95% right at first, then teach it the rest.
- **When you don't know how, ask the AI how.** Nathan's standing rule for his bots: "talk to me like I'm 14."
- **Build one block at a time.** Get one workflow working (a CMA in your MLS), then add the next (open Skyslope and start the paperwork). His showing bot started as "read the client email and map the houses."
- **Turn every working workflow into a skill.** Skills move between platforms (Grok, ChatGPT, Claude), so they double as your backup. Keep a personality document and a memory document per bot.
- Mistakes are training: show the right way once (e.g. the MLS number in the wrong Skyslope field) and it doesn't repeat it.
- AI copies workflow and logic, not charm. You still put your own personality in.

### Browser-control setup (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]])
- Search "ChatGPT Chrome extension," add it from the Chrome Web Store, pin it from the puzzle-piece menu. It opens a side chat that sees and drives the current tab. The Claude Chrome extension works the same way. Chrome only.
- **You log in yourself;** the extension operates whatever page is open. It controls the screen; it does not import your data into ChatGPT. "Any website you can log into, AI can take over."
- Cost argument: one 8-hour VA is at least ~$1,000/mo (24/7 coverage takes three); an AI plan is ~$20–100/mo. On a ~$13K average commission, one deal pays for ~11 years of a $100/mo plan.

### Listings, CMAs and paperwork (folds into [[Productivity]])
- **CMA:** with the MLS open, "Can you do a CMA on [address]," then "make that a PDF," then "make it match my brand."
- **Skyslope:** "Go into my email, find all my paperwork for [address], upload it, and create the transaction." "Start the listing paperwork for me" also works.
- **MLS data entry:** it fills the listing input and saves it as a **draft the agent approves** before anything goes live.
- **Market stats:** "last two weeks of stats in that area," then "turn this into a 9x16 for Instagram."
- **Showing bot:** forward the client's email listing homes to see; it books showings, plans the route (20 minutes per home plus drive time), researches each property and neighborhood, and sends the client a PDF.

### CRM and database (folds into [[Lead Generation]])
- In the CRM (Lofty, BoldTrail/kvCORE, Follow Up Boss): "Who are the top 10 most likely to transact in the next 30 days based on activity?" It used activity such as website visits and skipped contacts noted as already having an agent.
- Chain the next steps: "Email them a light touch, let them know I'm still here," then "Find and add them on my social media." It can also build a new internet-lead touch program and audit your website for SEO and AEO.
- **Facebook Marketplace buyer leads:** a bot posts one local listing a day with photos, answers inquiries and makes a matching story. Check MLS and brokerage advertising rules and get permission before advertising another agent's listing.

### Social media automation (folds into [[Social Media and Email Marketing]])
- **Engagement:** "Find 20 top realtors in Florida, follow them, like and comment on their posts" (comments specific to each post). LinkedIn: find local businesses and comment on their posts. It runs through your browser, so it looks like you.
- **Ramp slowly (Gogo):** 5 a day, then 10, then 20, up to 60 only once you trust it. Nathan still approves every comment and response while the bot learns.
- Post through the AI so it learns your voice. A content bot cut 54 podcast episodes into 190 captioned shorts and researches trending hashtags nightly.
- **Learn from courses and channels:** with a course open, "learn everything in this site about [topic] and turn it into a skill." His bot's read on Gogo's YouTube growth: copy "her willingness to be visible, her specific hooks, and her reuse of content."
- **Gogo's twist:** "Tell me what Gogo posted today about agent attraction, then duplicate it in my own words with my own call to action." Also: have the agent summarize GGTC trainings you missed and pick which to watch next.

### Bot teams (folds into [[Productivity]])
- Nathan runs ~26–28 Grok bots, each with one job (chief of staff that delegates, email triage, social media, deep research, real estate, podcast booking). He talks to them by voice while driving so they learn him. He teaches a 4-week cohort course on building them.

### Cautions (folds into [[Legal Protection]])
- **Review bulk sends.** His AI once emailed a showing confirmation to his whole ~2,200-contact database (he turned the apology into 3 listing appointments, but check before anything mass-sends).
- **Keep bots away from banking and payment details.** Attendees worried about sharing a Chrome profile with banking; treat it as a real risk.
- **Approval gates:** MLS listings as drafts, compliance check before advertising another brokerage's listing, approve comments and DMs while the bot learns.
- **Platform risk:** back up skills, personality and memory documents; pay for more than one AI (Gogo uses Claude and ChatGPT about equally). Free plans hit usage lockouts.

### Gogo's adoption advice (folds into [[Productivity]])
- **Block 2 hours a day** (8–10 AM for morning people, 8–10 PM for night people) and **automate one thing each day**. Daily, that's ~365 automations a year.
- Agents earn only from transactions and from bringing in agents. **Automate everything that isn't showings, open houses or negotiations.**
- You don't have to act on every new tool. Know it exists, then decide whether it fits how you want to run your business.
- Trainings are recorded and posted by the next business day; GGTC tech support (Tuesdays 3 PM ET) helps install the extension.
