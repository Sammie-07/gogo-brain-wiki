# The Circle Coaching — Year-End Tax Moves, Knowing Your Numbers and Delegating AI Setup (de-identified)

> Private [[The Circle|Circle]] coaching call (Nov 3, 2026, ~90 min). **NDA: raw transcript not retained; all member PII removed.** Only [[Gogo Bethke]]'s generalizable teachings are captured. Tax points are as Gogo stated them: "I am no CPA… talk to a professional."

## Key Takeaways

### Year-end tax homework (folds into [[Tax Strategy]] / [[Wealth Building]])
- November is the month to review the year by the numbers: two months remain to invest profit or lower taxable income, or "give it to Uncle Sam."
- **The list:** know your year-to-date income almost to the penny; know your tax bracket; cost-segregate investment properties; pay your children; max the 401k; open and fund an HSA; find your weaknesses and prepay coaches.
- **Prepay next year's coaches before Dec 31** to lower this year's taxable income. In the 37% bracket, every $1,000 she doesn't reinvest in herself sends $370 to taxes. She usually has two coaches a year, chosen by studying her weaknesses ("holes in your bucket").
- **Cost segregation:** an engineering study (~$3,500 per property; online estimate first, then an engineer visits) that usually moves 25–30% of the value into deductions now instead of over 27.5 years. Her example: a $300K rental with $60K down gives roughly $90K of deductions, about $33,300 back at 37%, while the $60K stays in an appreciating asset the tenant pays down. Only use it in a year you need the deduction.
- **Pay your kids (under 18):** up to $16,100 per child this year: up to $7,500 into a custodial Roth IRA, the rest into a regular investment account, opened and funded by Dec 31 (custodial accounts take longer to open). She has done it since her kids were small, and lets them research and pick stocks for part of it.
- **Contribution limits she gave (2026):** 401k $24,500 per person; HSA $4,400 individual / $8,750 family, by Dec 31. She skips the traditional IRA because, in her words, it doesn't lower her taxable income. Ask your CPA whether you qualify for the 20% QBI deduction.
- Other deductions she listed: home office, mileage, car payment.
- **Augusta rule:** the business can rent your home up to 14 days a year at a documented average daily rate, e.g. hosting a business event at home. A member's tip: lease your home to your business and run it through eXp as a lease transaction (~$250) so it counts toward ICON. Confirm both with a CPA.
- **6,000-lb vehicle lesson:** she wrote off a $180K Mercedes, sold it within five years, and owed tax on the recaptured amount, not just the small amount she netted. She now drives a Tesla Model Y on purpose: only a partial write-off, but self-driving gives her work time back.
- **Plan for the "broke" season:** year-end through Feb, maxed contributions, quarterly taxes, the April bill and a slow market hit at once. She maxes everything anyway.

### Knowing your numbers (folds into [[Wealth Building]] / [[Real Estate Math]])
- One tracker per LLC; each company's cards feed QuickBooks; reconcile statements against the spreadsheet monthly (she reviews a month around the 5th of the next).
- **New check:** a deposits-only report from each business bank account, Jan 1 to date, compared with the tracker, finds missing cards and income streams. Her review also caught an expense gap that changed a purchase decision for the year.
- Division of labor: [[Kristy Waker]] runs the business numbers; a personal assistant runs household numbers, updating stock accounts and each property's value and mortgage monthly.
- Property values come from HouseCanary CMAs inside eXp: free, five a month per account, better than Zillow in her view.
- **Asset tracker:** purchase price, current value, amount owed, monthly payment. Bad debt: a car bought at $80K, worth $50K, $65K owed, $750/month. Good debt: a $300K rental worth $420K, $160K owed, $2,000 payment, $2,200 rent. Equity is value minus mortgage, reachable through a HELOC up to 80% of value.
- Kristy offered one-on-one tracker setup so members' numbers stay private.

### An AI for every VA (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]] / [[Productivity]] / [[Downline Community Architecture]])
- The pace of AI change is exhausting: past tech waves let you be two years late; now a week is too late. Build AI for your weaknesses first instead of chasing every tool.
- Her stack: ChatGPT first, then Claude for the business side (trackers, automations) while ChatGPT stays on design; she pays for both. ManyChat runs keyword automations.
- **Each VA gets their own AI:** it researches the VA's field and sends a morning briefing, knows the job when they're out, and runs the routine work. Process: each VA updates their SOP (everything weekly, monthly, quarterly), the AI is built from it, then pull out whatever AI does faster. The team was comparing a one-bot-per-role tool with a single Claude setup.
- Claude automations took team workload from about 95% to about 60%; the target is about 25% human work, the rest freed for ideas or life. One team member automated about 80% of their own job.
- No staff yet: build AI versions of the roles you'd hire (marketing specialist, a CPA-style reviewer of your numbers).
- **Kristy's eXp MCP + Claude automations:** a new active agent triggers a text alert, GoHighLevel entry, welcome email, calendar/Drive/group invites and a gift-tracker update; transactions flow into the roster; offboarded agents leave the Facebook group monthly; the team training calendar syncs daily with the upline groups' calendars ("a whole another full-time job" before). She runs Claude in Chrome to double-check QuickBooks line by line.
- Idea for eXp agents: have AI watch the Hub and Facebook communities for referrals in your area.

### Get out of the details (folds into [[Real Estate Teams]] / [[Productivity]])
- Setting up software or AI is not the owner's job: know enough "to be dangerous," pay the subscription, have someone else set it up. "That's called the bottleneck." She has never set up ClickFunnels, GoHighLevel, ManyChat or the bot tools, and uses AI "like my therapist" for ideas.
- **Guard what goes out, not how it's made.** Review only the final result. Strip AI wording you'd never say until the team knows your voice. Approve every step at first; once work is consistently right, stop checking (she hasn't read her assistant Ferny's emails in months because they "sound exactly" like her).
- **Teams train themselves:** a new hire asks AI where to start building their role's bot. New hires get no tasks for 30 days; they sit in meetings and learn.
- "The smarter they get in their jobs, the more job security they have." She doesn't know how each employee does their job, and doesn't need to.
- Hands-off pairs with high standards: on-time meetings, trackers, number reviews, accountability; e.g. Black Friday ads due Friday, reviewed Friday, running the next week.
- Retention: Kristy 15 years ("we grew up together"), others 6 and 3+ years. When hiring fails it's the owner's fault: an unscreened referral hire, overpaying without researching rates, too burned out to train, or no consequences. Screen VAs for specific skills (GoHighLevel, ManyChat, Facebook groups).
- Her day: about 2–2.5 hours of work. "I've never worked this little and made this much money."

### Hourly rate and opportunity cost (folds into [[Real Estate Math]])
- "We don't do feelings decisions": fear of hiring is usually fear of doing the math. At $250/hour, one hour of your time pays for about 20 hours a week of a part-time VA; ~$1,000 a month buys roughly 80 VA hours. "You cannot save yourself to riches."
- Her own average is about $4,200 per working hour, so every hour spent figuring out something that isn't her job sets her back. Her loop: see a tool or idea on Instagram, send it to the team: "build this for me." "You are the big thinker. You are the leader."

### Learn to be bored (folds into [[Mindset]])
- Unlearn the belief that you're only worth something while grinding; free time doesn't make you lazy. Tony Robbins found it hard just to sit and chill with his son, a "professional chiller."
- Gogo calls this her "chill phase" after 15 years of working "like a mad woman": a Sunday of cooking, movies, a 5K and dog walks. Her brain still hunts for problems: "There are no problem. Stop looking for it."
