# The Circle Coaching — NDA Everyone, Golden-Handcuffs Hiring, Brand Architecture & the Freedom Arc (de-identified)

> Private [[The Circle|Circle]] coaching call. **NDA — raw transcript not retained; all member PII removed** (names, locations, brand/company names, incomes, split %s, family circumstances, and an extended personal/political/homeschooling discussion all stripped). Only [[Gogo Bethke]]'s generalizable teachings are captured. The user approved this category-level exclusion cut before writing.

## Key Takeaways

### Team building & hiring
- **NDA everyone who enters your world.** Anyone with access to your cloud, email, DMs, calls, notes, or physical space signs the same NDA — a Director of Operations, VAs, **even cleaning staff** (they hear every call and see your notes for hours). The reason: *you* are under many NDAs (masterminds, partnerships, business arrangements), so anyone in your world must honor them too, or they can't work for you. Use [[Claude]] to massage the template to your preferences. (Disclaimer Gogo repeats: "I'm no attorney.") Agency-sourced VAs already signed the agency's own contract; you just honor the agency's PTO. An ICA is optional and may not apply to overseas staff.
- **Always have a Plan B for key roles.** Even after hiring a Director of Operations, keep a **VA backup** so a sick day or vacation doesn't drop everything back on you. Two sourcing paths: **(a) find/train/hire yourself for free** (there are booklets: how to find/hire/train + how to pay them, and which groups to post in), or **(b) an agency** that finds + trains + gives a **90-day guarantee.** Gogo self-sourced for ~10 years, then switched to agencies ~1.5 years ago once recruiting became too time-consuming. "I never recommend a company or product I don't personally use."
- **The golden-handcuffs hire (the agent who can actually replace you).** A great agent who is *also* great at lead generation leaves you in ~6 months (they don't need you). The one who **stays** is elite at negotiation, super reliable, never a bad review, but **cannot lead-generate to save their life** — because they need *you* for the leads. That's the ideal hire. Use **DISC** to spot the profile, then train them into a **"mini-you at 75%" within ~6 months.** Tell Claude your timeline + each agent's strengths/gaps and have it build the training plan.
- **First 90 days accountable, then let closers close.** Run the trackers (lead tracker, greatness tracker) for the **first ~90 days** to build habits and reveal character. After that, as long as they close and honor your non-negotiables, **stop forcing data-entry on closers** — "closers and drivers don't do trackers"; hand it to a VA. Non-negotiables vary per person (e.g., "you must attend the one weekly meeting, because that's the only hour I get with you").
- **Delegate to strengths; stop doing low-value tasks.** The agent handles negotiation, marketing, and getting the client everything; **you don't attend inspections, closings, or walkthroughs** — pay a showing service (~$50) instead of driving an hour. *"You need me to negotiate and market and get you everything. You don't need me to twiddle my thumbs at a closing."* The blocker to freedom is the monkey on your shoulder ("I can do it better, I got burned before") — you have to be willing to give up the task.
- **Hunger is the hiring signal.** The best agents have their **back against the wall** (bills, kids, a real reason to chase the next commission). An agent with no responsibilities living at home has no hunger. (Caveat: hunger isn't only circumstance — a driven parent who trusts no one can outwork everyone.) Recruit by being your **authentic self on social media** so the right people find you: *"you need to know everyone who's selling, and they need to know you."* Keep the ask simple — "I need help, I'm looking for a buyer's agent to take leads off my plate, DM me" — then pick one good one out of a hundred; too many stated requirements scares people off (though it also sets a standard).
- **eXp standard-team split rule.** On a **standard team**, the team leader's **average** take must be **≥25%** (self-organized teams don't have this floor). You can take 60% from one agent and 20% from another as long as the *average* stays ≥25% — but you can't do 25% from one and 20% from another (avg 22.5% = you lose standard-team status). To reward a star **without** dropping the average: keep their 25% and pay them a **management override** (e.g., "you manage Jack, I pay you 5% of every Jack transaction") — they earn more, your average stays 25%. A management role + override is also the retention play for a star ("golden handcuffs" continued). See [[eXp Realty#Team Types]].
- **Digital products as attraction bait.** Build digital products you sell (e.g., $3,000) *or* give free when the agent brings their business to eXp — "either way, I made money." The product is the bait; the partnership is the win.
- **Slow season = build / train / switch season.** In seasonal markets, the dead months (can't show a house with Christmas gifts under the tree; nobody moves in deep snow) are for **building systems, training new agents** (ready for spring), CE, and vacation. Also advise agents to **switch brokerages in the slow season** — switching mid-busy-season with pendings + actives *and* learning new systems is overwhelming.
- **Leverage existing trainings instead of rebuilding them.** Schedule your team meeting so it doesn't collide with an existing upline training (e.g., meet at 12 so agents can attend the 1 PM training), and funnel your agents into trainings that already exist — it takes work off your plate.

### Brand architecture & positioning
- **Everything markets under your personal name, but each entity gets its own social identity.** Gogo runs *all* business under "Gogo's Real Estate" (transactions, coaching, community, the team org), yet **each sub-brand** (the team org, the community, the personal brand) has its **own** Facebook/Instagram. Your **downline/organization should have its own name and brand** — not just ride the upline's names. Why: if your marketing is *only* the upline's brand, a prospect can bypass you and go straight to the upline's leaders to get sponsored. Give your org its own identity + value (a monthly local mastermind, free monthly coaching, a monthly Zoom) so members belong to **your** organization *on top of* the upline layers — which also lets you separate the agents who are specifically with you.
- **Don't tie your org/brand name to a location.** eXp is international; a location in the title makes it hard for someone from another state to join you ("I had someone join from a state I've never set foot in"). Be the local expert without limiting the name to a location.
- **Your public-speaking / thought-leadership brand is your own name.** Build it under your name (like the Cardones, Robbinses, Vees) — "you won't find a public speaker who doesn't do it under their own name." You can still have legal entities/companies underneath (check any big speaker's website footer for their company names), but the *public* brand is you. Don't over-architect a separate company brand for your speaking; it grows into You.
- **Find the one word/pillar you can repeat forever.** Homework: identify the single concept you stand for — something you can "sell, pitch, and repeat like a broken record for the rest of your life," that stays true even if everything else changes.
- **Dissolution positioning: don't announce the split.** When dissolving a partnership or team, **don't explain it** — "you don't owe the world an explanation; the more you explain, the more attention and curiosity you create." Let the old brand **die a slow death**. Reframe around a neutral, positive reason (e.g., a marriage name change): *"same person, new last name, business stays the same, love you, can't wait to work with you."* And if you're rebranding at all, rebrand **fully**. (The dissolution's NDA + hold-harmless means you're contractually not allowed to discuss it anyway — "an easy fix.")

### Freedom, delegation & self-management
- **The 3-years-of-fire → freedom arc.** Gogo "burned the candle at all 1,700 angles" for ~3 years: stayed in production → built a local team to remove herself from production (~1.5 yrs to train buyer's agents, ~2 more to convert one into a listing agent = a ~3.5-year project) → then the broader team organization. Set a target **freedom date** and reverse-engineer it with Claude: "by this date I need my freedom, here's my team structure, tell me what to do *today* to get there" — which lets you compress the timeline.
- **Interrogate the goal.** Ask *why* you want the number (e.g., 25 listings): to work harder and add to your plate because you've got nothing else to do, or for the **money and freedom**? If it's freedom, plan to **give the listings up** once you hit the number.
- **Grow your audience on other people's podcasts.** Set a **fixed weekly slot** (Gogo did Tuesdays at 11) and guest on other people's podcasts to borrow their audiences — showing up consistently even when you don't know whose show it is. Have a VA do outreach with a **one-pager** (bio, accolades, a scheduling link locked to your fixed slot). After ~6–12 months, hosts start reaching out to you.
- **Events for brand-building.** Commit to a set number (e.g., 12/year) to get into the community and collaborate; start small, learn, scale to bigger events — some with "nothing to do with real estate," pure relationship/brand building.
- **Protect your wellness; live by design.** Schedule non-negotiable self-care (Gogo doesn't work Thursdays/Fridays — hair, nails, facial, the occasional massage). Find the rhythm **"between bat-shit crazy and burnout."** When you're fried, follow your heart and move a task to tomorrow rather than doing it exhausted and making expensive mistakes. The coach's repeated warning to an over-excited member: *don't burn out.*
- **The 12-week year** (member concept, endorsed): work in 12-week cycles, schedule backwards from the end result, and be intentional/faster — "get done in 12 weeks what takes normal people a year."
