# Expand Your Self-Image, Expand Your Life (Craig Siegel)

> [[Craig Siegel]]'s second GoGet'Em / #teamgogo mindset training (a ~60-minute session, delivered while he was traveling in Austin). Thesis: **self-image sets the boundaries for individual accomplishment** — you don't have a business/money/relationship problem, you have a self-image problem. Includes Gogo's fullest telling of the **100-agents / Rolex story** and her "God doesn't waste ideas" doctrine.

## Key Takeaways

- **Self-image sets your ceiling.** Your actions, tolerances, what you pursue/avoid, and even the opportunities you *let yourself receive* stay consistent with the image you hold of yourself. "You might not have a business problem. You might have a self-image problem." Two people with the same strategy, market, CRM, and brokerage create two completely different lives — because of who's interpreting it.
- **The self-image loop:** self-image → interpretation → action → result → **evidence → back to self-image.** We don't respond to reality; we respond to our *interpretation* of reality, then use the result as proof the original belief was true. "That's not who you are. That's who you've **rehearsed**." "**Familiar is not the same thing as true.**"
- **The listing-presentation example:** Agent 1 walks in thinking "hope they choose me"; Agent 2 thinks "let's determine whether we're the right fit." Different self-images → different physiology, questions, eye contact, tonality, relationship with rejection — and different odds of winning. If both lose, Agent 1 says "I knew it, I'm not established enough" (victim consciousness); Agent 2 asks "what can this teach me about my positioning?" Same rejection, completely different future.
- **The "area of possible."** Imagine a circle around you holding everything that feels *realistically* (not theoretically) available — the clients you can attract, the money you can comfortably receive, the room you believe you belong in, the price you're comfortable charging, the peace you can tolerate. Most people spend their lives trying to achieve *more inside the same circle* (new CRM, new funnel, new coach, more calls). "**You can't optimize your way around an internal limitation.**" The move is to **expand the circle**, not grind harder inside it.
- **Expansion is internal first.** Something that once intimidated you (first client, first $10K month, first stage) is now normal — the thing didn't change, *you* did. "Before your external life sustainably expands, your internal capacity has to make room for it." Even if something falls in your lap, without the vessel/capacity it gets sabotaged or doesn't last.
- **Don't lower the bar — expand the person under it.** (Workshopped with "Yvon.") Lowering the dream for a feeling of safety teaches your self-image that *fear gets to determine the size of your future*. Better question than "what if I fail?": "**Who could I become by attempting something I'm not guaranteed to succeed at?**" Failure = "collecting evidence, information, and capacity at a level I've never operated at." Would you rather **occasionally fail at the level of your potential, or consistently succeed beneath it?**
- **Sometimes self-image is too *old*, not too small.** (Workshopped with "Pam," who kept visualizing her 30-year-old self.) Your next self-image "doesn't need to look younger, it needs to look more like *you* — today, fully expressed." Everything you've lived is preparation, not evidence your best years are behind you.
- **The merchant parable (embody it before the evidence).** A shopkeeper succeeds by doing everything himself; opens a second store but keeps returning to run the first, so the second struggles — "expansion doesn't work." But he tried to build the second store *as the man the first store required.* First needed control/hustle; next needed trust/leadership. Creator's answer: "I didn't give you a larger opportunity so you can remain the same person inside it." We ask for abundance then cling to scarcity behaviors; ask for leadership then refuse to relinquish control. **Become willing to embody it before the evidence arrives** — not delusion ("pretending you have $10M when your account says $74 is bad accounting"), but becoming the person capable of *stewarding* what you asked for.
- **The Kabbalah frame (from Craig).** Creator/God/source "wants to give you everything" — that light is unlimited; what's limited is the **size of your vessel/capacity to receive.** Expanding the circle lets more of what's already being offered actually reach you. He anchored the session to Rosh Hashanah as "a cosmic opportunity to alter your destiny for the next 12 months," making the work stick harder.
- **Gogo's live doctrine:** God doesn't waste ideas; borrow no one else's picture of you; use doubt as fire.

## Gogo's Contributions (primary-source mindset)

- **God doesn't waste ideas.** "Every time God gives you an idea, you need to know in your gut a trillion percent that you got this… The when and the how will figure itself out." Fear just means you're trusting fear more than faith in that moment. Take action on every idea — to do otherwise wastes the talent God put in you.
- **The 100-agents / Rolex story (full telling).** When Gogo joined eXp she told her sponsor she'd bring 100 agents; he laughed, called her "honey," and said he'd buy her a Rolex if she did — because *nobody* had done it (eXp had ~36,000 agents then), so he figured he'd never have to pay. She'd picked 100 with no idea why; "it sounded easy." She only started doubting *after* he said no one had done it — then reasoned "why would God put that number in my head if it's not possible?" and went for it. "**Somebody else's inability to see your future does not make them an authority on it.**" Today she has the Rolex and has personally sponsored **300+** (grown into ~**1,700**).
- **Delusional optimism as an asset.** Craig calls Gogo "delusionally optimistic… the biggest compliment ever." Gogo: "I'm literally delusional and I love it all the way to the bank." "Delusion is just something nobody else believes in yet." You can observe limiting thoughts without giving them energy.
- **Use "you can't" as fuel.** "I love when people tell me I can't do something… I'll do it twice and post it." Don't borrow a critic's self-image of you — even when the critic is successful, an expert, or genuinely cares, they're interpreting your future through *their* area of possible.
- **The hardest work is on yourself.** "The reason you're not at the level is because you haven't taught yourself to accept that you can become that person." We love our comfort zones, don't want to outgrow people, tell ourselves it's lonely at the top — all justifications. "If we want a different future, we have to become a different human." Why coaches/mentors/events matter: we all have built-in blinders and need people who see the world differently to point out what we can't.
- **Age as a blessing** (to Pam): God gave you these years — many don't reach 60. "Go balls to the walls and find out who God created you to be." Better to look back and say "well, that was stupid" than "I wonder what it would have been."
