# The Circle Coaching — Post-Close Farming, Sponsor Onboarding and Brand Naming (de-identified)

> A private [[The Circle|Circle]] coaching call. **NDA — raw transcript NOT retained**; all member/team/vendor names, locations, and deal specifics stripped. Only Gogo's generalizable teachings are recorded here.

## Key Takeaways (generalizable)

### Post-close follow-up & neighborhood farming
- **Make follow-up a permanent step in the transaction process** — decide the tactic, define logistics (where to order, lead time, who executes), then "make it part of the process."
- **Walk the neighborhood around closing** with a low-pressure "welcome to the neighborhood / I just sold nearby" card → add contacts to the CRM → automated quarterly nurture drip.
- **Leave a "sold" sign up 1–2 weeks post-close** and add "your neighborhood realtor" yard signs where permitted; farm the same neighborhood repeatedly.
- **Direct mail:** "just sold / look what sold in your neighborhood" postcards to **300–500 homes** per drop (budget a few hundred dollars). **Door hangers/flyers** after a fast or over-asking sale: photo of the sold home + prior-year avg price/PPSF vs. your sale + a CTA. Never put anything in a mailbox (illegal — hang or hand it). **Caveat:** in seasonal/second-home areas, a door hanger signals an empty house — skip it there.
- **Facebook neighborhood groups you don't own:** offer to **sponsor an event** (e.g., a ~$200 garage sale) for permission to post a "did you know" PPSF update with a soft CTA.
- **Title-company data + email compliance:** a title rep can surface owner mailing/email addresses (occupants may be tenants). **Never mass-email scraped lists** (no opt-in → spam complaints can kill your email account) — send **individual templated emails ~8–10/day, no CC/BCC, from a separate address.**
- **Organic social at closing is the highest-ROI follow-up** ($0, wide reach): film a testimonial on the spot, **be in the video yourself**, tag clients and make them **collaborators**. Ask for a short marketing agreement to keep the collaborated post up **≥1 month**; reward with a modest gift ("even one commission pays for 100 dinners").
- **Post-close gifting as marketing:** give a **memorable personalized gift ~a month after closing** (custom home rendering, engraved "Established [family] [year]" board/doormat). **Branding it makes it a marketing write-off, not a gift** — many states cap the deductible closing *gift* (~$25), but a branded item (business info on a waterproof sticker or engraved) reclassifies as **100% deductible marketing** (state rules vary; see [[Tax Strategy]]).
- **Housewarming-party play:** ~2–3 weeks after move-in, host a small party at the client's new home (you supply food/drinks); meet their friends/neighbors for referrals. **Have the lender and title company chip in ~$100–200** each for food.

### Phone / number hygiene & boundaries
- **Don't retire a long-held business number for a "new" line** — the known number always rings; retraining a whole network is far harder than moving a few close contacts to a new *personal* number.
- **Better than a second phone:** a **CRM smart number that forwards** to whoever should answer, so your personal cell doesn't ring.
- Legit reasons for two devices: a "clean" content phone (auto-uploads photos/video to team cloud) separate from a personal phone; and being able to hand the work phone to an assistant and be present.
- **Get on the Do Not Call list**; **scrub your cell number off the internet** (site, sign photos, GBP, old posts) if you want spam to stop — realistically ~1.5 years of deletion to get near-zero.

### Sponsor onboarding system (recruiting/downline)
- **You own onboarding to *your* value-adds** even when corporate onboards brokerage tools — team trainings, resources, community, calendars, and referral map only happen if the sponsor does them.
- **Don't let recruits drink from a fire hose — stagger it:** wk 1 brokerage onboards → wk ~2 your benefits → wk ~3 broader upline/community → **wk ~4 a personal catch-up call** ("do you understand what you have, how to use it, and why?"). **Run overlapping onboarding calls separately** (combining them = "deer in the headlights").
- **A VA can run the *motion*; a long-tenured person owns the *relationship*** — recruits always know who to go to ("a partnership, not just a VA").
- **Application/hand-holding funnel:** an intake form (names you as sponsor/co-sponsor) → on submit, the sponsor + relationship-lead get an instant text and **start a group thread with the recruit from day one** (prospects take months and get cold feet — "1,700 buttons and nobody to talk to"). **Celebrate at "active"** with a personal welcome. Recruiting is "a lot of texting," not "where do I sign." (Sponsor-switch workaround: sponsor a different household member, e.g. a spouse.)

### Systems & entrepreneurial discipline
- **Entrepreneurs create chaos through their own great ideas** — install an **operating system** and a real manager/operator/team structure; slow down.
- **Every org needs a "no-man"** — if everyone's a yes-person, every idea gets chased in all directions; someone must ask "is that important *right now*?" and sort ideas from distractions.
- Recommended reading/course: a well-known **entrepreneurial operating system** methodology (visionary/integrator pairing).

### Brand / organization naming
- **Don't build the brand around your own name** — harder to market and scale (people wrongly assume a possessive "team" structure).
- **The name is for life — take your time;** avoid rotating industry buzzwords (they cycle every ~3–4 years); pick something crisp and enduring.
- **Crowdsource it:** ask trusted contacts "when you think of me, what do you think of?"; check names for prior use in your network first.

### Money / financial hygiene
- **Audit recurring charges** (pull a YTD vendor report) to cut/combine/renegotiate; **right-size subscriptions to actual usage** (annual plans may be locked until renewal).
- **"Respect your money" whether it's $19.99 or $19,000** — track it or it goes "poof."
- **Use the tools you already pay for** before buying new ones (an office suite often duplicates separately-paid scheduling/meeting tools).

## Quotes (general teachings)

> "Whatever you decide to do, make it a step to your process from now on."

> "We have to respect our money... if it's $19.99 you respect it; if it's $19,000 you respect it."

> "I have to do what I have to do until I can do what I want to do."

> "If everyone's a yes-man, then every idea is a great idea, and you're going a million miles in a million directions."

> "Don't build it around yourself. It's much harder to market it that way."
