# Top Negotiation Objections in Real Estate (Austin Rankin, Curt Shewell and Leila Modetz, GoGet'Em)

> [[GoGet'Em Community]] live training (~1h30m) run as a game show: a spinning wheel picks an objection and [[Curt Shewell]], [[Austin Rankin]] and [[Leila Modetz]] each answer in about 60 seconds. Hosted by [[Gogo Bethke]], with [[Kristy Waker]] running the wheel and timer.

## Key Takeaways

### The method (folds into [[Negotiation]] / [[Communication]])
- **Gogo's opening lesson:** talking a lot to sound smart is an early-career mistake. The shortest, most direct answer followed by a question wins, because whoever asks the questions runs the conversation. (Objection handling was hardest for her because English is her third language.)
- **Three styles:** Curt is assumptive and high-energy ("framing and looping": frame the client, then loop their answers back into the frame). Austin is purely question-led so clients reach the conclusion themselves. Leila stacks value and over-communicates (weekly seller updates).
- **Austin:** "Objections aren't to be overcome, they're to be understood." Find their reasons, not yours.
- **Curt:** work out whether it's an objection, a condition, or just something they felt they had to say. "Close with their words, not your words." If you don't understand: "Help me understand what you mean."

### Commission and fee objections (folds into [[Negotiation]])
- **"A discount brokerage will do it for 1%."**
  - Leila (value stack): list what your fee covers (videographer and photographer, Zillow Showcase, luxury marketing, 3D scan, open houses, door knocking), then: "3% for all of that, or 1% for someone you're probably never going to hear from after you sign. That's totally up to you."
  - Austin (question chain): "What's stopping you from hiring that agent? Why do you think they're so cheap? I don't think you want the cheapest agent; you want the one who nets you the most. If they can't negotiate their own income before you hire them, what could that mean for your equity?"
  - Curt: "What are you getting for that 1%? What if I was free?" (the buyer side funds it): "I get nothing until you get everything."
- **"Who pays you? I'm not paying out of pocket."** (buyer)
  - Leila: "I work for you. I'll ask the seller to cover as much as possible. If they don't cover it all, I'll ask you to cover the difference. Are you okay with that?"
  - Curt: "You're responsible for paying me, but you won't pay out of pocket. We put it into the offer and they pay me out of closing."
  - Austin: "What makes you believe you'd have to? Before we look at a property we make sure the seller is open to compensating a buyer's agent; if not, we add it into the price or look at a different home. I'm never going to let commission stand between you and the home you want."
- **The seller offers less than your fee:** have the conversation up front (Leila: "Are you okay covering the 1%, or do you want to keep looking?"); ask for a seller concession equal to the gap or write the price up, and spell the terms out so the other agent can copy them to their client (Curt). Austin: "The best negotiation comes with collaboration… it's not about you winning a war."
- **"Will you reduce your commission?"** Gogo: no. "If I can't negotiate my own commission, you shouldn't hire me in the first place." "We all get what we tolerate": if you floor at a lower rate, you set that standard yourself. Curt discounts only for genuine need, as charity work.

### Agreements and representation (folds into [[Negotiation]])
- **"How long am I locked in?"** Austin: "I don't think you want to be locked into anything, do you? Neither do I. Mind if I explain what the agreement actually does?" Then: "By the end of three months, what would have needed to happen for you to know choosing me was a good decision?" Leila matches the term to the buyer's real time frame. Gogo: "You can cancel any time, any reason, no cost, even because you no longer like my accent." Nobody ever cancelled; the easier and funnier you make it, the less it matters.
- **"Can we cancel if we're not happy?"** Yes, and you can fire a client too (Curt). Austin: "Have I given you any reason to believe you wouldn't be happy?… Let's be honest, we're going to be just fine, don't you think?"
- **"Why sign just to see a house?"** Austin: "Would you rather understand what I'm responsible for and how I'm paid before we walk into a home you love, or figure it out while you're emotional and racing another buyer?" Leila: "If you were a seller, wouldn't you want to know who's coming into your house?"
- **"I'll call the listing agent; they'll cut me a deal."** Austin: "The listing agent promised the seller the highest price. What will they pressure you to do?" Curt: "Would you let the prosecutor represent you?"
- **"I found it myself on Zillow."** Leila: "Send it to me," then pull the disclosures and check the facts (e.g. square footage that includes the basement). Austin: "Can you think of any risk in going without representation? Are those risks worth taking?"

### Pricing and listing objections (folds into [[Negotiation]])
- **"List it high; we can always come down."** Curt: "There are houses that sell and houses that help other houses sell." Price it to sell; "all I need is two people to want your home." Leila: monthly drops make buyers ask why it's still on the market and can cause appraisal problems. Austin's role reversal: "On day one with other buyers waiting, what would you offer? On day 90 with price drops and no competition? Which seller do you want to be?"
- **"Will you guarantee the sale?"** Ask whether they want a quick sale or the most money; guaranteed-buy offers are priced under market so the buyer can profit.
- **"We're interviewing other agents."** Austin: "How will you know you've found the right one? Do you mind if I go last, so you can compare? Can you promise you won't cancel our meeting?"
- **A seller who won't reduce after ~100 days:**
  - Gogo: a home doesn't sell for one of three reasons: price, condition or location, and after that long it's always overpriced. Market value is "what a willing and able buyer is willing to pay." Put a deadline on offers. If far off on price, walk; if slightly off: "In two weeks, without at least 10 showings, we reduce, and you have to allow me 'I told you so.'"
  - Curt: set the terms at listing (a higher price only for two weeks; without 10 showings it drops).
  - Leila: weekly reports with comps and showing feedback mean sellers raise the reduction themselves.
  - Austin: try to talk them out of selling to surface the real need ("What happens if we never sell?"); use a buyer's-agent feedback form so it's "not just me telling you"; "the pain of staying the same must exceed the pain of change."

### Buyer timing objections (folds into [[Negotiation]] / [[Lead Generation]])
- **"We're waiting for rates to drop."** Leila: buy now and refinance later; when rates drop, sideline buyers flood back and prices rise. Curt: "Marry the house, date the rate." Austin: "If you could get a great deal now without competing with other buyers, would you want that? Would it be a bad idea to put a Zoom on the calendar so I can walk you through a strategy?"
- **"Do I need pre-approval first?"** Gogo: "I don't want to break your heart." Curt: "Most people are approved for more than they think. Do you want to live in your second-favorite house?"

### Mindset of objection handling (folds into [[Mindset]])
- **Gogo:** people move toward pleasure or away from pain; know which from day one and "push that button" (e.g. sellers whose daughter is having triplets 150 miles away: "You want to miss the triplets over $3,000?").
- **Gogo:** "Not even Jesus could turn everyone into a Christian… I'm not everyone's cup of tea." When people "see the light" through how you respond to their objection, "you change their lives."
- **Austin:** "One of the biggest mistakes in communication is thinking it actually happened." On call anxiety: "Nothing to hide, nothing to gain, nothing to lose, nothing to prove."
- **Curt:** "When you ask the right questions, no is a good answer." People avoid calls because they fear questions they can't answer; asking the questions yourself fixes that.

### Gogo's sponsor story (folds into [[Co-Sponsorship and Agent Attraction System]])
- About 43 agents had pitched eXp to Gogo and she brushed them off. Her objection: moving brokerages twice in one year would make her look like a job-hopper ("I'm super loyal"). In August 2018 [[Curt Shewell]] asked one question: **"Who is your loyalty to: your brokerage or your family?"** She switched that day; she had put her loyalty on the wrong thing.
