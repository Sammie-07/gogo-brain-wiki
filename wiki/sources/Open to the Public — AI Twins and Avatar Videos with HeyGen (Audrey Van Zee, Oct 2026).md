# Open to the Public — AI Twins and Avatar Videos with HeyGen (Audrey Van Zee, Oct 2026)

> #teamgogo's monthly free [[#teamgogo|Open to the Public]] training (Oct 28, 2026, the last of 2026), hosted by [[Gogo Bethke]]. [[Audrey Van Zee]] (HeyGen community leader) teaches AI avatar videos; [[Jason Dupree]] gives the mortgage minute; [[Nick Krem]] covers getting found in AI search with [[Halo]]; [[Kristy Waker]] shares how the team builds Gogo's avatar.

## Key Takeaways

### Building an avatar (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]])
- An avatar has three parts you can swap independently: **motion** (a video of you), **looks** (photos), **voice** (an audio sample). Budget 1–2 hours of setup; it's an asset that keeps working.
- **Filming rules:** face a window, camera at eye level, look into the lens, neutral solid colors, plain background. **No props, big jewelry or hats**: the AI assumes you always have them. Gogo's first footage held a phone, which later flew out of her avatar's hand. "When in doubt, go back to neutral."
- Upload lots of photos; slightly rough ones avoid an over-polished look. Tattoos, rings and teeth still glitch.
- **Models (per Audrey):** Avatar 3 lip-syncs over a loop (~5–7 credits a minute, more robotic; fine as a small corner avatar); Avatar 5 is full-body with many looks (~50 credits a minute); Avatar 6 is in beta.
- **Voice:** try each engine; Kristy found Cartesia worked best for Gogo. HeyGen Voice adds similarity, expressiveness, speed, tone and accent settings. Ask someone who knows you which version sounds most like you. Workaround: lip-sync the avatar to your real voice note. One account can hold several voice clones (e.g. two languages).

### Making videos (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]])
- **Video Agent** builds a full storyboarded video (B-roll, captions, graphics, music) and shows its plan before spending credits, so correct the plan first. **AI Studio** edits scene by scene and uses the fewest credits for small fixes; move a video there if a Video Agent chat "loses the plot."
- **Real estate templates:** listing videos, market updates, neighborhood spotlights, education, multilingual. Turn them on under Settings → Personalization → "Real estate and property." A zip code alone produced a usable market update; a listing link alone can make a home tour.
- **Let AI write your prompts:** fill HeyGen's starter prompts in ChatGPT or Claude, then paste the result into Video Agent. More input (your own photos, links) gives better output.
- **Brand settings:** give it your website for colors and fonts. The **Brand Glossary** fixes pronunciation (type entries or import a CSV); Gogo's team added entries spelled the way she says words.
- HeyGen can also connect to Claude or ChatGPT, so videos can be made without opening HeyGen.

### Prices and credits (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]])
- Creator plan: $29/month (~600 credits, roughly 10 simple one-minute videos); first month free for Team Gogo through the call's code. Pro: ~2,000 credits a month (Audrey believed ~$99). Credits are spent only when a video is generated; building the avatar costs none.
- Start monthly, adjust month to month, and go annual once usage is steady. Gogo: long YouTube videos use more credits, so start free and upgrade once you know your style's cost.

### Disclosure (folds into [[Social Media and Email Marketing]])
- **Always disclose:** tick Instagram's "AI was used" box. People are "neutral, curious" when you explain why; friction comes from feeling tricked. "As humans, we don't like to be tricked."
- Cut a version per platform. Avatars remove three blockers: confidence, production cost, keeping up with distribution, and they enable other languages.
- "This isn't necessarily about going viral… It's about staying relevant, staying in front of your audience."
- HeyGen's agent case studies (vendor material): a daily AI-news reels account that grew its following severalfold; YouTube agents cutting production from 5 hours to 2 and closing deals from video leads.

### Getting found by AI search (folds into [[Lead Generation]] / [[Personal Branding]])
- [[Nick Krem]]: a [[Halo]] site turned local questions (AnswerThePublic, Google, Reddit) into FAQs and blog posts plus an "Ask [agent]" page linking back to them; an out-of-state agent found him repeatedly on Claude, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, leading to two $1.5M listings. Tell your "AI CMO" to blog 3×/week and keep updating FAQs. Halo: first 5 edits free, then $99/month.
- **MLS news (Nick):** MLSs are blocking unapproved AI tools and will approve specific connectors; check what yours allows.
- **Gogo's do-it-yourself method:** define your ideal client and area, ask Claude or ChatGPT for the 3–10 most-asked local real estate questions, and write ~10 blog posts answering them. "The easiest way to create, guys, is copy the one that you love": have AI rebuild a site you like in your own brand.
- Gogo: "I don't care how many reviews you have on your company-provided website, you're not even a choice, because AI cannot search that site."

### The program (folds into [[Co-Sponsorship and Agent Attraction System]] / [[Wealth Building]])
- Open to the Public runs the last Wednesday of the month, free and open to anyone (eXp or not); Wine Not eXp is the same night at 8 PM ("no hard sale"). Invite undecided prospects so you can be "co-sponsored together." Agent-attraction and co-sponsorship websites are built in 2–3 minutes.
- **REMLO:** with 6 buy-side transactions you're paid 0.5% on the mortgages on deals you already do (this month's highlighted REMLO earned an extra $1,700 on one closing). "I always work really hard to bring multiple sources of income for everyone." REMLO business planning in December is open to all agents.
