# The Circle Coaching — Achievers Live in the Future, a Busy Producer's Agent Attraction System and a Grok Bot per VA (de-identified)

> Private [[The Circle|Circle]] coaching call (Nov 10, 2026, ~54 min), the week after Tony Robbins' UPW. **NDA: raw transcript not retained; all member PII removed.** Only [[Gogo Bethke]]'s generalizable teachings and her own team facts are captured.

## Key Takeaways

### Achievers live in the future (folds into [[Mindset]] / [[Wealth Building]])
- Ask yourself: do you spend your thinking time in the past, the present or the future? Achievers live in the future, chasing what they don't have yet, and never slow down to ask "What do I really freaking want? What is an ideal day?"
- Her example: someone upset she wasn't a millionaire by 30. Gogo did the math with her: two homes, retirement accounts and about $500K reachable through HELOCs. "You already are a millionaire. You just never slowed down enough to do the math."
- "Everything comes at me when I leave town" is a belief the universe proves right. Say "I am going to meet 10 people this week," not "I'm hoping to."
- Tony Robbins' line, applied to speaking: "We are millionaires, they just haven't paid us yet." You are a professional speaker who hasn't had the big stage yet.
- UPW is her yearly reset: it takes her out of the everyday so she can imagine the life she wants. "You charge your soul batteries, and then you get to go for another year."

### Agent attraction for a busy producer (folds into [[Co-Sponsorship and Agent Attraction System]])
- "It's not that you have a time problem, you have a priority problem." Busy transaction weeks become the excuse to skip agent attraction unless it is scheduled and the team covers it.
- **One local event a month, same day every month** (e.g. the last Wednesday), planned a year ahead by the assistant: holiday themes (a Santa party in December, a red-carpet Valentine's event) and learning topics in quiet months (lending, title, AI). Outside experts can teach, but you speak at least once a quarter: "that makes you an authority." Agents you meet on the other side of deals always have something to be invited to.
- **One weekly coffee block** at the same shop (e.g. Friday 9–11): invite agents, coffee is on you, and real estate comes up naturally, including your stock or rev-share payout on the laptop.
- **Agent attraction posts on fixed days** (the member chose Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday; production content the other four). The assistant sets a 9 AM reminder; two posts a week can be pre-created and scheduled. If you're at a closing on attraction day, the team still posts for you.
- Gogo's own path: one agent attraction post a week while she still needed commissions, more as rev share grew. Posts "are out there forever"; a coffee isn't.
- **Don't "eXp all over them."** Play it cool, call switching "a big decision," and text your booking link. When they book the call and watch your page's videos first, they're serious; otherwise there's no natural second step. Keep the link saved in your phone notes.

### Hire out what you don't love (folds into [[Real Estate Teams]] / [[Productivity]])
- "If you love what you do, you never work a day in your life" doesn't mean shut down the part you hate. It means hire it out.
- "You cannot be doing data entry activities and wanting to make a million dollars." At a high hourly rate you're "better off facing the corner and picking your nose for an hour."
- A member's lesson on hiring VAs: interview several, score them on a scorecard, and tie each hire to a trackable revenue-producing activity; then hiring three costs less fear than hiring one. Ask candidates for their conversion numbers and offer a closing bonus.

### Gogo's team and a Grok bot for everyone (folds into [[AI for Real Estate]] / [[Real Estate Teams]])
- **Team in her words (Nov 10, 2026): [[Kristy Waker]] plus seven VAs, all full-time.** Gogo works about 5–10 hours a week; her fixed commitments are the Monday team meeting and the Circle call.
- [[Kristy Waker]] built a **Grok bot for every team member** from their SOPs and Trello tasks (already connected to Claude): one bot per person that is both assistant and job coach, keeps them on schedule, and searches daily for ways to do the job better. Each person connects their own Google, calendar, Drive, Zoom and Trello, which a single shared Claude account couldn't do. Kristy's bot even researched and filled the cart for team t-shirts; she keeps payments manual.
- Gogo's capacity math: VAs full-time plus their bots running 24/7 plus her few hours ≈ **1,794 hours of work a week**, against 40 for an average person. She is the only one without a bot "because I don't actually do anything."
- **Cross-posting:** she posts once on Instagram and a bot reposts within about 5 minutes to LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok and all her other accounts. "Kill 27 birds with one stone."
- **Cost lesson (member):** Claude usage climbs fast when many connectors are attached, because each task runs through them all. Limit each workflow to the 3–4 connectors it needs, or run routine work on a lighter model.
- Her team is safe from AI: "I need humans to set up the newest technology that's coming." VAs are upgrading their homes because they feel secure; several are at about five years. "There's not one rotten tomato on my team."

### Learn to relax (folds into [[Mindset]])
- "We have chased for so long that we don't actually know how to relax without feeling guilty," and the guilt drags you back into the grind.
- Homework: take four hours off on your busiest day this week. No DMs, no thinking about the business. "You all need to learn to trust your team. But you do have to have a team."
