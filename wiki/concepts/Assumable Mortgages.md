# Assumable Mortgages

A buyer takes over (assumes) a seller's existing low-rate loan instead of originating a new one at current rates — a lead-generation and affordability lever in a high-rate market. Surfaced in [[#teamgogo]] via [[AssumeList]].

## Core Idea

When rates are high, a home carrying an assumable **sub-5% loan** (many at 2.4–3% from 2020–21) is dramatically more affordable than the same home bought with a new ~6.25% loan. The rate, remaining balance, and servicer stay the same; the buyer steps into the seller's shoes. Marketing an assumable listing ("get this house at 2.43%") drives inbound buyer demand — agents who promote assumables reportedly grow fast.

## Which Loans Are Assumable

Only **FHA, VA, and USDA** loans (not conventional).
- **FHA:** any creditworthy buyer may assume (needn't qualify for a specific FHA program); must live in the home the first 12 months.
- **VA:** assumable even by non-VA buyers and **investors** — if the veteran leaves the tied **entitlement** behind (a VA entitlement calculator quantifies this). A key investor angle: VA loans are the rare assumable path open to non-owner-occupants.
- **USDA:** assumable but restrictive — the assuming buyer must meet USDA rules, notably income **≤115% of local median household income**.

## The Equity Gap (Second Mortgage)

Because prices rose and principal was paid down, the buyer must cover the gap between purchase price and the remaining loan balance. In practice the buyer needs **~10% down**, and any amount above that can be financed with a **second mortgage** — but many lenders don't offer them for assumptions, so a specialized second-mortgage lender matters.

## Savings & Reality

- Monthly savings today are ~**$500–$1,000** (were $2,000–$3,000 a few years ago); some buyers pursue assumptions to save even ~$100/month.
- **Why banks stall:** servicers are legally required to allow FHA/VA assumptions but keep those teams hidden/understaffed — they'd rather write a new full-rate loan than transfer a 2.7% one. A DIY assumption usually fails; a specialist ([[AssumeList]]) navigates the process, and all liability transfers seller→buyer in a federally regulated process.

## Agent Playbook (via [[AssumeList]])

- Market assumable listings with a **green-screen video** in front of the listing photo (address whited out), leading with the rate.
- In covered MLS areas the listing is **auto-added** to the marketplace once on the MLS (if the loan is assumable and below 5%).
- Fees are paid by buyer/seller, not the agent (a flat TC fee; no charge beyond a small setup fee if the assumption isn't approved). See [[AssumeList]] for specifics.

## Ground Truth vs. Bank Misinformation (Nora Simpson)

The regulation-based truth: **all VA and FHA mortgages are federally required to be assumable at the seller's request, regardless of servicer, if the buyer is creditworthy. Full stop.** Google, Gemini, and ChatGPT return **half-truths** on assumptions — because banks fund the noise. When a low-rate loan is paid off, the bank re-lends at 6.7–6.9%, so **banks pay thousands of bloggers to spread confusing/conflicting information** and their reps (and AIs) illegally claim "we don't do assumptions."

## The Four Obstacles (and how a specialist solves them)

1. **Hard to find** — owners don't know they have an assumable loan; agents don't know to ask or which loans qualify.
2. **Marketplace misconceptions** — the bank-funded misinformation above.
3. **Bank obstruction** — the runaround, plus **manual underwriting** weaponized: for a *new* 6.7% loan a bank uses fast automated approval, but for an assumption it does line-by-line manual underwriting to find a reason to decline (sometimes miscalculating DTI). The fix is regulatory pressure — [[AssumeList]] corrects the math and escalates to the **VA Inspector General** (which once threatened a bank's license to service $1T+ of VA debt → 25-day closings after).
4. **Cash-gap financing** — see below.

## Cash-Gap Financing: Primary + Secondary

The cash gap = purchase price − assumable loan balance, and it **grows every month** the seller keeps paying (a "burning platform" for urgency). The buyer covers it with a **primary + secondary strategy**: get pre-approved for a normal loan (conventional/VA/FHA) at today's rate to finance the gap, layered on the assumed low-rate first.
- **Example (Houston, VA 2.65%, $129K gap, 10% down):** saves **>$500/mo**, **blended rate ~4.2%** vs. 6.3–6.7% market. Many homeowners say they'd move if rates dropped below 5% — a blended 4.2% clears that bar.
- Preferred gap lenders: **Ryan & Kelly at Barrett Financial** (AZ; 49 states, not NY).

## VA Entitlement Math (why non-VA buyers and investors can assume VA loans)

- **Entitlement can be split** — leaving it behind on one property doesn't burn all of it.
- Entitlement tied to a property = the **original loan amount** (or refinance amount), NOT the remaining balance; both are public info. AssumeList shows a **VA entitlement calculator on every VA property.**
- **2nd-use VA funding fee:** 3.3% (zero down) → **1.5% at 5% down** → **1.25% at 10% down** — a reason to put some cash down on the *next* home.
- **The 4× rule:** the VA guarantees the bank 25% of a VA loan, so a vet buying above their remaining entitlement can **self-guarantee with cash = 25% of the gap**. Putting ~$99K down can unlock **~$396K more buying power** (e.g., an ~$808K VA purchase). County loan limits vary (all TX ≈ $832K in 2026; CA/VA/NJ/NY higher).
- **Investor angle is VA-only:** leaving entitlement behind means the **12-month owner-occupancy is already satisfied**, so an investor can assume. An **FHA** assumption **restarts** the 12-month owner-occupancy — a non-starter for investors.

## Who It's Actually For

Assumables are for **financially-qualified people to save money**, not a no-down path for buyers with no cash. Target the **73% of homeowners who would move today if they could keep their rate** (2026 Yahoo Finance survey); **80% of homeowners hold a sub-6% rate**, and **99% of realtors have never closed an assumption** — so a newly-trained agent instantly out-knows 99% of their market. Prospecting play: the AssumeList **mail-campaign** tool sends handwritten "golden letters" to owners of assumable homes (filter by rate/type, skip-trace contact) — see [[Lead Generation]].
