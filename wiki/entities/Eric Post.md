# Eric Post

Founder and chief architect of [[Halo]], the AI website agency agents run by text. A veteran real estate operator turned AI builder who demoed Halo live for #teamgogo on Sept 2, 2026 with [[Nick Krem]].

## Overview

Eric Post is "not a tech guy" by background — he says he's **sold thousands of houses personally and done $4B+ in real estate transactions** through his team, and has owned real estate brokerages (multiple Northwest brands), mortgage companies, land development, and home-building services. He is a former **combat engineer in the U.S. Marine Corps**. He built Halo to solve being found by the AI-powered consumer, and frames the mission as "low calories in, maximum dopamine returned" — minimal effort from the agent, maximum output.

## Key Facts

- **Company:** Founder / chief architect of [[Halo]]. Based in Happy Valley, Oregon.
- **Real estate:** $4B+ in transactions via his team; former brokerage/mortgage/land-dev/home-building owner.
- **Military:** Combat engineer, U.S. Marine Corps.
- **Connection to Gogo:** Spoke at **Project 42** (AI event in Canada organized by Nick Krem); met Gogo there; brought into #teamgogo via Nick.

## Core Teachings

- **Skate to where the puck is going** — the AI-powered consumer, not competitors-with-AI, is the disruptor in a knowledge-based service industry.
- **Efficiency is table stakes; the real question is surprise, delight, and value delivered** — not just "how fast/cheap can we do it."
- **Own your platform (authority graph), don't rent it** — social is rented traffic at the mercy of the algorithm; get people into an ecosystem you own.
- **Content = friction points/life triggers → education → why you.** "If you don't know what you stand for, how will the internet know how to recommend you?"
- **How AI works:** LLMs "produce plausible language, not fundamental truth" — know how it works to apply it well. In prompting, "it cares more for clarity than pleasantries."
- **Just start:** "Get your toe in the water… iterate your way to the truth." "You can't build the second store as the person the first store required" (echoes the [[Craig Siegel]] merchant parable).
